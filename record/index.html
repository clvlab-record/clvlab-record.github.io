How each number is measured

Every published signal, unedited. CLV has no single industry definition — what it is compared with, how the margin is removed, when the close is taken and which entry price is used all change the number — so each row shows three measures, each defined below.

Sharp CLV (our definition, used for the counts above): the sharp book's last price before the start, margin removed conservatively (the lower of multiplicative and Shin, so longshots are not flattered), divided by the published entry price, minus 1. Fees are not deducted.

Market CLV (anyone can check it): the same venue's own last price before the start — the middle of its best bid and ask — divided by the published entry price, minus 1. No outside data; the same definition on every venue. Shown from 2026-10-15.

Lines. A line is venue × strategy × market. Taker: bought before the start when the price clears the bar against the sharp no-vig line, sold once the market catches up (Kalshi cannot be traded from here: its signals are recorded and settled the same way). Hold: the same scan, held to the result — for it the Result is the outcome that counts. Moneyline vs Spreads/Totals: the market type. Every line is numbered 1–100 and counted on its own; only Polymarket · Taker · Moneyline is the primary hypothesis.

Result (Hold): whether the side won, once the market settled (Void = settled 50-50) — shown for Hold lines only, which are judged by it. Taker signals are sold before the start and judged by CLV alone, so their rows show n/a. A single result says little — judge the averages.

Each venue has its own entry price, fees and closing price, so the same game can show different numbers on different venues. Each venue is numbered and counted on its own; the rows are never merged.
